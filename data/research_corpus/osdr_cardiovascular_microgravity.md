# Cardiovascular Remodeling and Hemodynamic Redistribution in Microgravity

## Hemodynamic Shifts and Cephalic Engorgement
Upon entry into microgravity, approximately 1.5 to 2.0 liters of interstitial and vascular fluid shift headward from the lower extremities to the central thoracic and cephalic compartments. This cephalad fluid shift produces early facial edema, nasal congestion ("space stupor"), and jugular venous distention.

## Cardiac Output and Resting Vitals Adaptation
Initial central blood volume expansion stimulates cardiac baroreceptors, temporarily increasing cardiac output and transiently suppressing plasma volume via diuresis and reduced renin-angiotensin-aldosterone activity. Within 72 to 96 hours, plasma volume decreases by 10% to 15%, establishing a new baseline resting heart rate and arterial pressure in long-duration crewmembers.
