# Risk of Performance Decrements and Disorders Associated with Sleep Loss and Circadian Shift

## Microgravity Sleep Disruption
Astronauts on long-duration orbital missions consistently average approximately 6.0 to 6.5 hours of sleep per night, significantly below the nominal 8.0-hour requirement. Contributing factors include microgravity somatic discomfort, acoustic disturbances from life support ventilation fans, and rapid 90-minute day-night orbital cycles.

## Circadian Desynchrony and Cognitive Performance
Chronic circadian desynchrony leads to cumulative sleep debt, diminished vigilance, slower reaction times, and elevated reports of fatigue. Microgravity alters melatonin secretion rhythms and circadian core body temperature cycles. Astronauts experiencing sleep deprivation exhibit increased subjective headache susceptibility and reduced workload endurance.
